The system

Small enough to remember

Today the code has 84 hex colors, 31 font sizes, 15 corner radii and 303 hand-made buttons. This is the whole replacement.

Six rules

Every screen above follows them.

  1. One yellow button per screenIt's always the next step. Everything else is black, white or a text link.
  2. Color stays smallMuted pastels only in status pills, dots and progress bars. Surfaces stay white; modules get an icon, not a color.
  3. Three shapesPills for anything you tap, 20px cards, 12px for things inside cards.
  4. Six text sizes11 · 13 · 15 · 18 · 24 · 40. No half pixels.
  5. Drawn icons, not emojiOne marker-weight line per idea, the same pen as the illustrations.
  6. Words come from LEXICON.mdNo module numbers on screen. Sentence case.

The style

Drawn like your reference

Meet Myco, the MyCoNet mushroom (mycelium is where the name comes from), on the way to a circle meeting with a coffee and a seedling.

What I see in your reference

And what every drawing in MyCoNet copies from it.

  1. One thick, even lineLike a felt-tip marker, round ends. Never thin-to-thick, never sketchy.
  2. Drawn, not constructedNo perfect circles or ruler lines. Bodies stretch tall, things bulge and lean.
  3. Few lines, lots of airOnly what you need to read the shape. No shading, no texture.
  4. One joke per drawingSunglasses, a smug mouth, an x-eye. A wink, never a cartoon scene.
  5. Two colorsCream line on cobalt. Solid fill only for tiny accents, like the shades.
  6. LayeredThings overlap and hide what's behind them; big cropped type sits behind the character.

Where it goes

Illustrations (cobalt, with a character) for welcome screens, empty states, project and event images. Icons use the same line without the joke, in ink on the light UI.

Icon review

Drawn, one per idea

Every icon MyCoNet needs, drawn with the same marker as the illustrations: one thick, even line, round ends, shapes that lean a little. Ink on light in the app, cream on cobalt in illustrations. They replace all emoji, the module numbers and the per-module colors.
